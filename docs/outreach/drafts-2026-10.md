## 1. Rhythm & Brains Lab, UCLouvain (Prof. Sylvie Nozaradan)
Lab page: https://uclouvain.be/en/research-institutes/ions/rhythm-brains-lab

**Subject:** Field data on movement–music synchrony in Brussels clubs: collaboration?

Dear Professor Nozaradan,

I'm building Viibe Check, a research app that measures, from phones in people's pockets, how a crowd moves with the music in real nightlife settings. Each phone records only summary numbers: movement energy and tempo, how consistently movement locks to the beat (phase-locking value), the room's tempo and loudness, and an anonymous 1–3 rating of the moment. No audio is stored.

Your lab's work on rhythm perception and body movement is the closest scientific grounding for what we are trying to measure outside the lab. I'd value your view on two questions:
1. Are our beat-synchronisation measures (phase locking against the heard beat and against a wall-clock grid at the song's tempo) sound, and what would you change?
2. Would a student project or a small collaboration interest your group? We plan a pilot with 15–25 students in Brussels city centre clubs this autumn and can share the anonymised dataset.

A 20-minute call would already help a lot. I can send a one-page summary of the method beforehand.

Kind regards,

## 3. IPEM, Ghent University (Prof. Marc Leman)
Relevant work: DanSync, a social music game to study joint action in dancing (https://eudl.eu/pdf/10.1007/978-3-319-03892-6_15)

**Subject:** Measuring dance synchrony in real clubs with phones: interested in field data?

Dear Professor Leman,

Your group's work on embodied music cognition and joint action in dance (DanSync, among others) is the reference point for a project I'm running in Brussels. Viibe Check uses the phones in people's pockets to measure, in real clubs, how each person's movement locks to the beat and how much people move in sync with each other beyond what the shared music explains. People also rate the moment (💀/🙂/🔥) a few times per hour.

The central question is whether this collective synchrony predicts how much people enjoy the night better than simple loudness or crowd size. We plan a pilot with 15–25 students in Brussels city centre clubs this autumn.

Would your group be interested in the resulting field data, or in advising on the synchrony measures? A short call would already be very valuable. I can send the method summary in advance.

Hi C12 team,

- We bring the testers: a group of 15–25 students. A small perk for them (a drink, guestlist on one night) would help, but it's optional.
